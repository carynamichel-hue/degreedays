🌡 Degree Days pest scouting by growing degree-days

📖

🌡 What Degree Days is

Degree Days tells you which nursery pests are active now, which are coming, and where to scout — from the temperatures at your own location. Insects develop with heat, not with the calendar, so a running total of growing degree-days (GDD) since 1 January says where each pest is in its life cycle. The pest list and its thresholds come from the Rutgers Nursery & Landscape Pest Scouting Guide (2023); every row is editable and you can add your own.

It is free, has no account, and there is no server. Everything you enter lives in the browser you enter it in — back it up (⚙ Setup) if you care about it.

What it is not: a spray schedule. A threshold means "go and look", not "go and spray". The guide's ranges are for scouting, may not hold outside the region they were observed in, and the plants in your yard always win over any model.

1 · Two minutes to running

  1. Open ⚙ Setup and search for your town under Locations ("Bridgeton NJ" works; a street address does not). Pick the result.
  2. Weather fills in by itself: Open-Meteo gives every day since 1 January and a two-week forecast. Wait a few seconds.
  3. Open 📊 Now. The big number is your season total; below it are the pests that are Active now, Coming soon, Later and Passed.
  4. Back in Setup, under Pest categories at this location, untick what you do not grow (no conifers, no fruit…). Those pests leave the lists for this location.

2 · The Now screen

  • Accumulated degree-days: confirmed days from the biofix through today, plus the forecast days in lighter type. "N per day lately" is the recent rate, used to date the coming events.
  • Active now — the pest's stage is inside its degree-day window today. Each line names the stage to look for, the window, how much of it is left, and the reference behind it.
  • Coming soon — the next stage is within the "coming soon" distance (⚙ Setup, default 150 DD) or 14 days at the recent rate, with an estimated date.
  • 🔍 Scout lists the crops or blocks you typed on the pest; otherwise the guide's favored hosts.
  • 📆 on a line puts that one event in your calendar; 📅 View as of in the bar rewinds the whole app to any past date, which is the way to see what May looked like.
  • With several locations, the card at the top compares them and switches between them.

3 · The Season chart and calendar

  • The curve is the season's running total, solid where confirmed and dashed where forecast, with a dot on the day each pest's threshold was or will be crossed (grey passed, green active, amber coming; hollow = estimated). Hover or tap a day for the detail.
  • Zoom to 3 months, 1 month or 2 weeks, or drag across the plot with a mouse. Last year draws the previous season in grey dots for comparison. All locations overlays every location's curve.
  • The calendar shows each day's degree-days and total, with the pest events as chips. Tap a chip for its detail and a one-event calendar export; 📆 .ics exports the whole month; 📷 saves either card as a picture.

4 · Pests and categories

  • 🐛 Pests lists every pest with its stages. Edit opens the row in place: hosts, your crops or blocks to scout, base and upper temperatures, the stages and their degree-day ranges. + Add a pest creates your own; Restore the Rutgers list puts the seed rows back without touching yours.
  • Categories (folded at the top of Pests, and on Setup) are the groups a location can switch off — conifers, broadleaf evergreens, deciduous, fruit, perennials, many hosts — seeded from each pest's hosts. Rename them, add your own, and tick the pests that belong to each.

5 · Weather sources

  • ☁ Open-Meteo is on by default: a weather model's analysis for your coordinates, worldwide, no account. Good enough to start; a few degrees off on some days.
  • Your station beats the model for the days it measured. Attach one per location: a 🛰 NEWA station (find the nearest, or Compare these stations to see which one's highs and lows behave like your site), your own 🔌 LI-COR logger by API token (the token stays in this browser only), or the nearest 🦅 NWS station. A day from a station counts only when it has at least 20 hours of readings.
  • 📄 Import a file: a daily sheet (date, max, min) or hourly readings from any logger. Typed temperatures win over everything.
  • The 📋 Forecast check below the sources grades every source against what actually happened here — how far the highs and lows ran, the degree-days per day, the season total difference — over 30 days, 90 days or the season, with an Excel export of every day.

6 · Backup, sharing, Excel, phones

  • 💾 Download backup is your only copy: locations, sources, pests, categories, daily temperatures, settings. Restore it here or on another device; it merges, newer wins, nothing is deleted.
  • 🔗 Make a link or a share file hands a colleague your locations, the pests you edited or added, and your categories — they fetch their own weather. Opening a link shows what would arrive before anything lands. Neither ever includes a LI-COR token.
  • 📊 Excel: this location's daily temperatures and degree-days, the pest list with its events, or everything.
  • 📱 On a phone, open the link and "Add to Home Screen". It opens without signal on what the device already has, says so with a 📴, and refreshes when you are back online.

7 · Where the numbers come from, and their limits

  • Degree-days use the simple average method with a horizontal cutoff: the day's high and low are each capped at 95 °F, averaged, and 50 °F is subtracted; a day below 50 adds nothing. That is the method the Rutgers thresholds were built on and the one the Pesticide app's Degree Days tab uses. °C is display only.
  • Accumulation runs from 1 January (the NEWA and USPest convention). Change the biofix only if your extension office says so.
  • Forecast days are counted in the totals so "coming" events get a date; they are shown in lighter type and never more than 14 days out.
  • Pest data: Rutgers Nursery & Landscape Pest Scouting Guide, beta 2023, T. Waller, NJAES — for scouting purposes only. If your observations differ from the guide, the authors ask to hear about it at twaller@njaes.rutgers.edu.
  • The guide itself compiles degree-day ranges published by several institutions. Every stage in the app carries the number of the source it came from (shown as "Ref." on the Now screen and "[n]" on the Pests tab):
    1. Rutgers University
    2. Cornell University & University of New Hampshire
    3. Penn State University
    4. Michigan State University
    5. University of New Hampshire
    6. University of Maryland
    7. Michigan State University
    8. University of Delaware & North Carolina State
    9. PA Department of Agriculture
